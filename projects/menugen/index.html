MenuGen / Prototype

Pictures are a kind of translation

The home laboratory · 1 min read · 19 Oct 2026

A dish name can be perfectly legible and still tell you very little. Recognizing the words is different from being able to imagine what will arrive at the table.

MenuGen explores that gap. It takes a photograph of a menu, uses a vision model to extract the items, expands their descriptions, and generates dish illustrations. A React interface sits over a FastAPI backend, with model requests going through LiteLLM-compatible services or an image-generation provider.

The workflow has several places to go wrong. Text extraction can misread an ingredient. A rewritten description can add something the menu never said. Image generation can then turn that invented detail into a convincing picture. Each stage depends on what the previous one preserved.

That makes the distinction between an illustration and a restaurant photograph essential. The prototype can help someone form an impression of a dish. It cannot establish its ingredients, allergens, portion size, or actual appearance. Those questions still belong to the restaurant.

What interests me is the possibility of making unfamiliar choices easier to approach. A generated picture can serve as a translation into something immediately understandable, as long as the interface leaves room for the uncertainty behind it.
